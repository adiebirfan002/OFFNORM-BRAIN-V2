# FPA (Future Pattern Archive)

Guangzhou, China. Began 2018 as VENGETICE, renamed FPA in August 2020. Streetwear that moved into sport.

Labels: **[S]** sourced, **[M]** from memory (verify), **[R]** my read. Sources in [sources.md](sources.md).

**Honest note first:** FPA is the thinnest of the four studies. English coverage is small and most Chinese
coverage is retailer or promo copy. FPA is not a running brand; it is a sport-and-street label with a running and
trail line. Treat it as a reference for *branding mechanics*, not for running credibility.

## The one-line idea

Clothes as entries in an archive of the future: sport function, street shape, worn all day.

## Origin

- Started in summer 2018 in Guangzhou as VENGETICE by two founders known as JACO and RAS. The old name joined two
  opposite words and stood for raw street impulse. **[S]**
- Renamed FPA in August 2020; VENGETICE kept as a keyword inside the brand. **[S]**
- Grew online first. Named one of Taobao's top ten Chinese streetwear shops in 2020. First pop-up end of 2021.
  Permanent stores from 2023 in Shanghai, Chongqing, Chengdu and Xi'an. JD.com flagship April 2025. **[S]**

## Narrative

- **Name**: Future Pattern Archive. The brand describes itself as a recorder that files away the path of growing
  up, with each garment as one slot in the archive. Stated aim: design that outruns the present moment. **[S]**
- **Style words used about it**: light function, new deconstruction, retro-futurism, sport-outdoor. **[S]**
- **Store story**: "Four cities, four stores". Each shop is one archive entry, built from the same hand-made
  glazed brick but in a different colour and local theme (yellow bamboo in Chengdu, green ridges in Chongqing).
  "Connecting past and future." **[S]**
- **International site lines**: "For People in Action" (the letters F-P-A re-read), "Performance without the
  uniform", "Technical where it matters, effortless everywhere else", "Built for everyday motion". **[S]**

## Visual identity

- **Logo**: three capital letters, FPA, used as a compact block on chest, sleeve and hem, usually with the full
  name or a line of small text beside it. I could not find a reliable written description of the letterforms and
  did not see the mark itself, so do not rely on this file for its exact shape. **[M, weak]**
- **Graphic language [M]**: sports-team and motorsport cues: numbers, colour blocking, stripes, small
  technical text, sponsor-style patches. Capsules are built like team kits ("Soccer Capsule Series").
- **Colour**: black, white, red, navy, mustard; colour-block, contrast panels, vintage and heathered washes. **[S]**
- **Stores as product**: the design firm says the store is the brand's most representative product. **[S]**

## Product

- Performance tees, active and trail shorts, shells, fleece, down. Cotton-lyocell and Pima cotton sit next to
  Coolmax and PrimaLoft in the same range. **[S]**
- Running pieces include 3-inch two-layer trail shorts and marathon styles. **[S]**
- Product names on the web shop are keyword strings, not names. Named lines exist ("Arc", "Aurora"). **[S]**
- Prices roughly US$85 to US$350 on the international site. **[S]**

## Voice

Short uppercase headlines, verbs first. "Layer light, move easy." Functional, confident, no romance and no
jokes. **[S]**

## What OFFNORM can take

1. **Three letters that re-expand.** FPA means Future Pattern Archive and also For People in Action. An acronym
   becomes a copy machine. OFN can do the same (see [../brand/logo-brief.md](../brand/logo-brief.md)).
2. **"Archive" as a filing system.** Number every drop, every piece, every run. Numbering makes a young brand look
   like it has a past and gives collectors something to follow.
3. **Kit logic.** Design a drop like a team kit: number, block colour, small sponsor-style text. It reads as sport
   without a single performance claim.
4. **Cotton and technical fabric in one range** with no apology. Useful proof for a cotton-led running label.
5. **Same system, local colour.** One fixed element everywhere, one thing that changes by place.
6. **A homegrown Asian brand at near-global prices**, built online first. The closest business model to OFFNORM
   among the four.

## What not to copy

The word "archive", the retro-futurist racing look, and keyword-stuffed product titles. Also the weakness: FPA's
story is vague next to the other three. It has a look and a system but no clear enemy and no clear belief.
OFFNORM should take the mechanics and bring a stronger reason to exist.
