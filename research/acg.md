# ACG (Nike All Conditions Gear)

Beaverton, Oregon. Official launch 1989. Nike's outdoor brand; since 2026 it also holds Nike's trail running.

Labels: **[S]** sourced, **[M]** from memory (verify), **[R]** my read. Sources in [sources.md](sources.md).

## The one-line idea

One promise in three words: gear that works in all conditions. Said with a grin.

## Origin

- **1978**: two climbers on the K2 expedition, Rick Ridgeway and John Roskelley, walked the 110-mile approach in
  yellow Nike LDV running shoes instead of boots. They posted the worn shoes back to Nike with notes: tougher
  sole, tougher upper, keep it light. **[S]**
- **1981**: Nike Hiking launches with the Lava Dome, Approach and Magma: running technology on rough ground. The
  Approach was Nike's first Gore-Tex shoe. **[S]**
- **1984**: the Escape, Nike's first proper trail running shoe. **[S]**
- **1987**: a small team under Tom Clarke and Kirk Richardson is told to pull the scattered outdoor products
  together. They set four principles: high performance, durability, versatility, functional excellence. **[S]**
- **The name**: "All Conditions Gear" was already being used by Nike Running for a small all-year apparel line.
  The outdoor team took it because it said what they meant. **[S]** So ACG's name began in running.
- **1989**: ACG launches as shoes and apparel together. First catalogue shot from a helicopter at Castleton Tower,
  Moab. **[S]**

## Eras

| Years | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1989 to late 1990s | Cult era. Tinker Hatfield and Peter Fogg design the Air Mowabb (1991), Deschutz sandal, Air Moc, Air Terra, Humara. Loud colour, funny ads **[S]** |
| Late 1990s to 2000s | Folded into the main Nike business, loses its edge **[S]** |
| 2014 to 2018 | NikeLab ACG with Errolson Hugh of ACRONYM: black, taped, city techwear **[S]** |
| 2018 to 2025 | Back to heritage under James Arizumi: retro shapes, colour returns **[S]** |
| 2026 | Full relaunch. Nike Trail is folded in. "All Conditions Racing Dept" of about 30 trail runners. Team USA wears ACG at the Winter Games with no swoosh **[S]** |

## Narrative

- **Belief**: light is right. A running shoe could do a boot's job. Less is more, "functional minimalism". **[S]**
- **Enemy**: stiff, serious, heavy outdoor brands made for middle-aged hikers. A 1992 Mowabb ad is described as a
  middle finger to them. **[S]**
- **Promise**: one line, never changed in 37 years. All conditions.
- **2026 framing**: the outdoors is now high-aerobic and fast, not slow. Unplug, get outside. **[S]**

## Voice

This is ACG's biggest asset after the name.

- Tongue-in-cheek, sometimes plainly funny. Long-copy ads with jokes in them. **[S]**
- Examples reported: "Not everyone was willing to wait for our hiking boots" (over the K2 photo), "Call of the
  really really wild", a "Top Ten Reasons to Start Outdoor Cross-Training" list. **[S]**
- 2026 relaunch film made with a co-creator of Jackass, with nature-documentary narration played for laughs. **[S]**
- Retailers credit the "edgy slogans" for the cult following. **[S]**

**[R]** ACG is funny about itself and serious about the product. It never jokes about whether the gear works.

## Visual identity

- **Logo**: three letters locked inside a triangle, a shape that reads as a mountain and as a warning sign. It
  works at any size, in any colour, embroidered or printed, with or without the swoosh. **[M]**
  One reseller source credits the triangle to Rick Lohr and says a rounder "lungs" logo replaced it from 1997 to
  2002; I could not confirm either. **[S, weak]**
- **Colour**: taken from places. The Mowabb's sand, lake and rock tones came from Utah. Purple, teal, orange,
  speckled midsoles. In the 2014 era, all black. In 2026, a bright orange train. **[S]**
  **[R]** The logo survives every palette change because the shape is fixed and only the fill moves.
- **Naming**: products are named after real places. Mowabb (Moab), Deschutz (Deschutes river), Huascarán, Mount
  St. Helens, Okwahn, Wildwood. **[S]** The map becomes the product catalogue.
- **Typography**: bold condensed sans in capitals, long paragraphs of copy on the back of tees and in ads. **[M]**

## Product as proof

Developed in Nike's sport research lab with motion capture and climate chambers; tested by climbers first, then
by a racing team. Products can mark a moment as well as perform: the Mount St. Helens jacket carried a
before-and-after patch of the mountain. **[S]**

## What OFFNORM can take

1. **Three letters in a fixed container.** This is the "ACG shape" the founder asked for. The container does the
   recognising; the letters can be drawn plainly.
2. **A name that is also the promise.** ACG never needs a tagline. OFFNORM's name already holds a stance; use it
   the same way and stop looking for a separate slogan.
3. **Humour as a brand asset.** Almost no premium running brand is funny. Satisfy is romantic, norda is quiet.
   The gap is open, and Malaysian humour is a real local strength.
4. **Name products after real places.** For OFFNORM that means Malaysian routes, hills, parks and roads.
5. **Colour from the place.** Not "brand colours" picked from a chart: tones taken from where you run.
6. **Light is right** suits the tropics more than it ever suited Oregon.

## What not to copy

The triangle, the swirl, the purple-teal-orange set, and the phrase "all conditions". A triangle with three
letters in it will be read as ACG by everyone who matters.
